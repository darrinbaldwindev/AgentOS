# AgentOS Commercial Validation Plan — 2026-09-07

**Owner:** Marketing Overseer  
**Status:** RECOMMENDATION / HYPOTHESIS / VALIDATION REQUIRED  
**Scope:** Free → $29/year → $99/year commercial proposition, 30-day onboarding, Night Shift/autonomy positioning, and message-market fit.

## Purpose

Convert the current AgentOS commercial direction into testable hypotheses with explicit pass/fail evidence. This document does not approve pricing, claim product-market fit, or assert that all described capabilities are implemented.

## Current working commercial ladder

### FREE — Taste / Assistant
**Message:** Get productive with AgentOS.

Role: give a deliberately limited but genuinely useful taste of the AgentOS experience. Free should prove the core mental model — tell AgentOS what you want done — without becoming the complete product.

### $29/year — Co-worker
**Message:** Work with AgentOS.

Role: the natural low-friction paid progression at about $0.56/week. The user receives materially more capability, delegation, context, connections, AI resources and limited scheduled autonomy.

### $99/year — Operator
**Message:** Put AgentOS to work.

Role: intended mainstream destination for users who derive ongoing value from AgentOS. The proposition is materially greater scope, capacity, automation and autonomy within user-defined authority.

### Connected subscriptions / BYO AI
**Message:** Give AgentOS more capability.

Role: serious users can extend AgentOS with paid AI, specialist services, APIs, local compute and business systems subject to provider terms, permission, policy, budget and availability.

## Commercial rule

Sell outcomes, delegation and autonomy — not plugin counts.

The underlying free/premium/local/provider capability pool remains strategically important, but the customer-facing question is:

> **How much useful work can AgentOS take off my hands?**

## Primary hypotheses to validate

### H1 — Core proposition
**Candidate:** `You already have AI. AgentOS helps you make the most of it.`

Supporting line: `One platform. Every AI.`  
Supporting idea: `Make the most of AI.`

**Pass signal:** novice users understand within 10 seconds that AgentOS coordinates AI/tools so they do not need to choose models manually.

**Fail signal:** users interpret AgentOS as merely another chatbot, model aggregator or AI directory.

### H2 — $29 conversion bridge
**Hypothesis:** $29/year feels like a natural, low-risk upgrade from Free rather than a major purchase decision.

**Pass signal:** users can articulate at least two reasons to upgrade that are about useful work, not abstract model/plugin inventory.

**Fail signal:** users see no meaningful difference from Free or view $29 as a dead-end tier.

### H3 — $99 destination
**Hypothesis:** $99/year becomes compelling when framed around greater autonomy/scope rather than simply more AI resources.

Primary message test:

> **AgentOS can work for you, on your schedule.**

**Pass signal:** regular users understand why $99 is materially more valuable than $29 without needing technical explanation.

**Fail signal:** users describe $99 as merely “more credits/models”.

### H4 — Night Shift
**Hypothesis:** `Night Shift` is a mainstream-friendly explanation of scheduled autonomy.

Test language:

> **Give AgentOS work before bed. Wake up to the results.**

**Pass signal:** users understand both the benefit and that AgentOS remains bounded by permissions.

**Fail signal:** users perceive it as unsafe, intrusive, always-on, or technically complicated.

### H5 — Character onboarding
**Hypothesis:** Willow, Isla, Jack and Henry improve comprehension and trust when introduced through actual useful work rather than tutorial cards.

- Days 1–7: Willow — plans.
- Days 8–14: Isla — does.
- Days 15–21: Jack — protects.
- Days 22–30: Henry — checks.

**Pass signal:** users can explain the four roles in plain language and understand they are one AgentOS team rather than four separate products.

**Fail signal:** characters add confusion, feel childish for the target audience, or obscure what AgentOS actually does.

## First message test

Test three top-level propositions against the same product demonstration.

### Variant A — Resource-led
**Headline:** You already have AI. AgentOS helps you make the most of it.

**Support:** Bring together the AI you already use and let AgentOS choose appropriate capability for the job.

### Variant B — Delegation-led
**Headline:** Tell AgentOS what you want done.

**Support:** AgentOS plans the work, uses the tools you allow, gets the job done and shows you what happened.

### Variant C — Autonomy-led
**Headline:** Put AI to work.

**Support:** Delegate work to AgentOS and let it continue within the boundaries and schedule you choose.

### Decision rule
Do not choose the winner by preference alone. Measure comprehension, trust, activation intent and willingness to pay.

## 30-day Free onboarding test

### Days 1–7 — Comprehension
Goal: user understands “tell AgentOS what you want done.”

Measure:
- first useful task completed;
- time to first useful result;
- user can explain Willow's role;
- return within 7 days.

### Days 8–14 — Delegation
Goal: user experiences Isla completing useful steps.

Measure:
- multi-step task attempted;
- task completion;
- interaction reduction versus manual workflow;
- user can describe what $29 would add.

### Days 15–21 — Trust
Goal: Jack demonstrates control rather than friction.

Measure:
- approval comprehension;
- confidence that AgentOS will stop at authority boundaries;
- permission completion rate;
- trust score before/after approval experience.

### Days 22–30 — Assurance + upgrade
Goal: Henry demonstrates checked work and the product shows the user's accumulated value.

Measure:
- evidence/assurance interaction;
- usefulness of “How do you know?”;
- $29 upgrade intent;
- $99 future intent;
- reasons for upgrade/refusal.

## Night Shift marketing test

Show a simple workflow:

1. **Tonight's Queue** — Willow shows planned work.
2. **Night Shift** — Isla executes authorised work.
3. **Jack holds** anything requiring approval.
4. **Henry checks** completed work.
5. **Morning Brief** — completed, blocked, failed and needs-attention items.

Test against generic language such as `background automation`.

**Primary success condition:** users prefer and better understand `Night Shift` without incorrectly believing it grants unlimited authority.

- approval comprehension;
- Stop/Pause discoverability;

### Conversion
- Free → $29;
- $29 → $99;
- time to upgrade;
- upgrade reason;
- refusal reason.

### Retention/value
- 30/60/90-day retained use;
- jobs completed;
- workflows reused;
- useful work completed during scheduled windows;
- factual AI usage/cost metrics where available.

## Claims guardrail

Marketing must not claim:

- guaranteed savings;
- guaranteed best/optimal model selection;
- unlimited free AI;
- implemented Night Shift/autonomy before runtime evidence exists;
- cryptographic proof unless actually implemented;
- enterprise-grade capability at $99 without evidence;
- provider availability, limits or commercial rights without current verification.

## Required evidence before locking the commercial model

1. At least one novice comprehension test for each top-level message variant.
2. Real willingness-to-pay evidence for $29/year and $99/year.
3. Evidence that the Free tier is useful but leaves a natural upgrade reason.
4. Evidence that $29 materially improves perceived capability.
5. Evidence that $99 autonomy/scope is understandable and desirable.
6. 30-day character-onboarding comprehension/trust test.
7. Night Shift comprehension/safety test.
8. Actual cost/capability modelling against the verified capability registry before promising included AI volume.
