# AgentOS Member Referral Programme — Validation Test

**Owner:** Marketing Overseer  
**Date:** 2026-09-07  
**Status:** HYPOTHESIS / VALIDATION REQUIRED

## Purpose

Define the smallest credible member-get-member referral programme test for AgentOS. This is separate from the creator/affiliate programme and is intended for ordinary users who have already experienced real product value.

The objective is not to manufacture referral volume. The objective is to determine whether satisfied users naturally want to recommend AgentOS, whether a modest reward improves that behaviour, and whether referred users activate and retain at acceptable quality.

## Core hypothesis

AgentOS can create a healthy referral loop if users reach a genuine value moment first, then receive a simple invitation to share it with someone who would benefit.

Desired word-of-mouth behaviour:

> "Instead of figuring out which AI to use, I just give AgentOS the job."

This is a desired organic user description, not an approved marketing claim or guaranteed user sentiment.

## Separation from creator / affiliate programme

### Member referral
For ordinary users referring friends, family, colleagues or peers.

Possible reward types:
- extra AI allowance;
- extra Night Shift runs;
- temporary Operator capability;
- account credit;
- limited premium capability period;
- another low-cost/high-perceived-value benefit.

### Creator / affiliate
For creators, publishers, reviewers, newsletters, media, communities and commercial partners.

That programme may use attributed commission economics and must remain independently governed.

Member referrals must not silently convert ordinary users into affiliates or require them to make commercial claims.

## Referral eligibility trigger

Do not ask for a referral immediately after signup.

A member referral prompt should require a demonstrated value event first. Candidate eligibility triggers for testing:

1. 10 completed AgentOS jobs; or
2. 3 separate active days; or
3. 1 successful Night Shift / scheduled result reviewed; or
4. 1 explicit positive feedback event; or
5. a completed high-value workflow followed by continued use.

The final trigger should be selected by observed referral quality rather than prompt-volume optimisation.

## First test cohorts

### Cohort A — no reward
Prompt after value event:

> "Know someone who would find AgentOS useful? Invite them to try it free."

Purpose: establish natural referral propensity.

### Cohort B — capability reward
Possible test:

> "Invite someone who activates AgentOS and you both receive extra AgentOS capability."

Candidate reward: extra AI allowance or limited Night Shift runs.

Purpose: test whether product-native rewards increase qualified referrals without creating excessive cost.

### Cohort C — temporary premium experience
Possible test:

> "When your referral activates, you both receive a short Operator capability boost."

Purpose: test whether a temporary premium experience produces both referral activity and later $39/$99 conversion.

Do not test all reward variants simultaneously in a small cohort if doing so makes results uninterpretable.

## Referral landing experience

The referred user should land on a simple page with minimal terminology.

Working structure:

**You were invited to try AgentOS.**

**Put AI to work.**

AgentOS helps bring AI and your tools together so you can give it the job instead of figuring out every model and tool yourself.

**Try AgentOS free.**

Do not lead with Willow / Isla / Jack / Henry, Everyday / Essentials / Tech Head, model catalogues, routing architecture or affiliate mechanics on the referral landing page.

## Referral share copy candidates

### Candidate A — natural recommendation
> "I've been using AgentOS to get AI jobs done without worrying so much about which model to use. You can try it free here."

### Candidate B — outcome-led
> "AgentOS lets me give AI a job instead of jumping between different AI tools. Thought you might find it useful."

### Candidate C — minimal
> "Try AgentOS — put AI to work."

The system should allow the member to edit or replace suggested copy. Never force or auto-post promotional claims.

## Reward design principles

1. Prefer rewards with high perceived user value and low marginal cost.
2. Reward qualified activation, not raw link clicks.
3. Do not encourage spam or mass unsolicited outreach.
4. Avoid cash rewards for ordinary members during the first test unless economics and abuse controls justify them.
5. Make reward conditions visible before the user shares.
6. Never make the member responsible for claims AgentOS itself cannot substantiate.
7. Do not make referral economics influence AI/provider/model routing.

## Candidate qualifying event

A referral should count only after the referred user completes a meaningful activation event.

Working candidates:
- completes 3 real tasks; or
- returns on a second active day; or
- completes first meaningful workflow; or
- reaches another verified activation milestone.

A mere signup should not necessarily trigger full reward because it creates low-quality and abuse-prone acquisition.

## Anti-abuse / quality requirements

Before activation, implementation should support controls such as:

- no self-referral;
- no duplicate-account farming;
- account/device/payment/address heuristics where appropriate and privacy-compliant;
- reward caps;
- reward delay until qualifying activation;
- reversal where fraudulent activation is confirmed;
- rate limits on invitations;
- clear prohibition on spam;
- no automatic contact-book harvesting or messaging;
- user-controlled sharing only.

These are product requirements, not claims that such controls currently exist.

## Metrics

The referral test should measure:

### Referrer behaviour
- eligible users shown referral prompt;
- prompt acceptance rate;
- invitations/shares per eligible user;
- repeat referral behaviour;
- reward-driven vs natural referral rate.

### Referred-user quality
- referral landing conversion;
- signup rate;
- activation rate;
- second-day / seventh-day retention;
- first-week completed jobs;
- $39 conversion;
- $99 conversion;
- upgrade from $39 to $99;
- support/abuse incidence.

### Economics
- reward cost per activated referred user;
- equivalent CAC;
- paid conversion contribution;
- gross-margin impact;
- reward breakage / non-redemption;
- downstream retention quality versus non-referred users.

## Success interpretation

Do not set arbitrary permanent success thresholds before baseline data exists.

The first test should answer:

1. Do genuinely activated users voluntarily refer others?
2. Does a reward materially increase qualified referrals?
3. Are referred users as good as or better than ordinary acquired users?
4. Does the programme strengthen $39 / $99 conversion rather than only Free growth?
5. Is reward cost materially below other viable acquisition channels?
6. Does referral prompting damage trust or feel spammy?

## Preferred initial reward hypothesis

**RECOMMENDATION FOR TESTING:** Start with a product-native two-sided reward rather than cash.

Example test concept:

> "Invite a friend who becomes an active AgentOS user. You both receive extra AgentOS capability."

Candidate capability reward:
- referrer: additional AI allowance or Night Shift runs;
- referred user: temporary access to a higher-capability experience after activation.

Exact reward quantum remains UNKNOWN until unit economics and product-capacity costs are available.

## Relationship to pricing

The member referral programme should support the current pricing test:

- Free — useful taste;
- $29 control vs $39 challenger — Co-worker;
- $99 — Operator destination.

Referral rewards should not make the paid tiers unnecessary. The strongest test rewards should therefore expose premium value temporarily or increase usage capacity rather than permanently unlock the full Operator experience.

## Upgrade interaction

A referral prompt is appropriate after the user experiences value. An upgrade prompt is appropriate when the user reaches a capability/capacity/autonomy boundary.

Do not stack both prompts at the same moment unless testing establishes that it improves rather than damages trust.

Example sequencing:

1. user completes valuable workflow;
2. AgentOS acknowledges completion;
3. user continues using AgentOS;
4. later value event → referral prompt;
5. later paid-boundary event → upgrade prompt.

## Message hierarchy

Top-of-funnel:

> **Put AI to work.**

Consideration:

> **You already have AI. AgentOS helps you make the most of it.**

In product:

> **Tell AgentOS what you want done.**

Referral word-of-mouth target:

> **"Instead of figuring out which AI to use, I just give AgentOS the job."**

## Evidence boundary

- No member referral programme is claimed to be implemented.
- No reward economics are approved.
- No referral conversion rate is known.
- No claim that AgentOS is "the best way to access AI" is approved without evidence.
- No member should be encouraged to present an unsupported superlative as an official AgentOS claim.
- All reward structure, qualification logic, fraud controls and economics require implementation and validation before launch.

## Next marketing decision after first test

If organic referrals occur even in the no-reward cohort and referred users activate well, prioritise the member referral loop as a core acquisition channel.

If referral activity appears only when rewards are unusually generous or referred users are low quality, keep referrals lightweight and prioritise creator/affiliate and other acquisition channels instead.
