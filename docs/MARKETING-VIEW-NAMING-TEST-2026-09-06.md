# AgentOS — Experience View Naming Test — 2026-09-06

**Owner:** Marketing Overseer  
**Status:** HYPOTHESIS / VALIDATION REQUIRED  
**Scope:** Product marketing and customer-facing terminology only. No runtime, entitlement, permission or architecture authority.

## Decision context

The customer-facing experience needs a name for the least-complex presentation of AgentOS. The existing term **Basic** is rejected as a strategic marketing term because it can imply reduced capability, a lesser product, or an entry-level version.

The governing product principle is:

> **One AgentOS. Different views.**

Every view represents the same full AgentOS capability. The difference is how much system detail, configuration and control is surfaced to the customer. The views must not be marketed as capability tiers.

## Current view model

| View | Working purpose | Capability position |
|---|---|---|
| Candidate replacement for Basic | Lowest-complexity, mass-market presentation | **100% AgentOS** |
| Essentials | More visibility for regular/involved work | **100% AgentOS** |
| Tech Head | Deep visibility and control for technical/power users | **100% AgentOS** |

## Naming candidates

### 1. Everyday — preferred working candidate

**Why it works:** communicates ordinary, frequent use without implying lower capability. It supports the mass-market objective and makes progression feel like increasing visibility/control rather than upgrading the intelligence itself.

**Customer-facing concept:** “Everyday — the simplest way to use all of AgentOS.”

**Risk:** may sound like a usage frequency rather than a view or product mode; requires audience testing.

### 2. Simple — strong clarity, weaker brand distinction

**Why it works:** immediately communicates low complexity.

**Risk:** can still be interpreted as a simplified or reduced product. It is also less distinctive as a named experience.

### 3. Personal — strong consumer signal, but potentially too narrow

**Why it works:** makes AgentOS feel approachable and individual.

**Risk:** may incorrectly imply that the experience is only for personal use, conflicting with the portfolio-wide consumer-to-business ambition.

### 4. Natural — approachable, but abstract

**Why it works:** suggests an effortless interaction model.

**Risk:** does not immediately communicate what changes between views.

### 5. Open — positive, but ambiguous

**Why it works:** can suggest openness and approachability.

**Risk:** may be confused with open-source, open models or open access.

## Recommended test hierarchy

**Preferred:** Everyday  
**Control:** Simple  
**Alternative:** Personal  
**Exploratory:** Natural / Open

This is a marketing recommendation, not a locked product decision.

## Positioning rule

Avoid language such as:

- Basic version
- entry-level AI
- limited AI
- starter intelligence
- reduced capability
- upgrade to get the real AgentOS

Prefer language such as:

- the simplest way to use AgentOS
- the same AgentOS, your preferred view
- choose how much you want to see
- start simple, reveal more when you need it
- **One AgentOS. Different views.**

## Commercial architecture interaction

The experience-view system must remain separate from the commercial progression:

**Free → $29 → $99 → subscription** is a commercial/value journey.  
**Everyday → Essentials → Tech Head** is an experience/visibility journey.

They must not be presented as interchangeable ladders. A customer can use the full AgentOS in any view and move between views without the marketing implying that intelligence or safety has been withheld.

The pricing names and exact entitlements remain commercial hypotheses until independently validated and approved.

## Trust implications

The view name must not weaken or hide the trust story. Across views, messaging should preserve the same core expectations around:

- security and privacy claims only where evidenced;
- local-file handling only where evidenced;
- quality/review behaviour only where evidenced;
- safety boundaries and governed actions only where evidenced.

## Validation plan

Test the top three candidates — **Everyday, Simple, Personal** — with at least:

1. immediate comprehension: does the person understand what the view is?
2. capability perception: do they assume it is full AgentOS or a reduced tier?
3. audience fit: does it feel suitable for ordinary users?
4. progression fit: does it coexist naturally with Essentials and Tech Head?
5. commercial neutrality: does it avoid implying a pricing/entitlement tier?
6. trust fit: does the name support rather than undermine security/safety messaging?

**Validation status:** no customer evidence yet. Do not claim a winning name until tested.

## Next action

Use customer-facing copy tests and, when available, real-user feedback to compare Everyday vs Simple vs Personal. Keep **Everyday** as the current working candidate only; do not rename implementation/UI surfaces until the naming decision is explicitly approved.
